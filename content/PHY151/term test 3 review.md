## 1

Two blocks, $A$ and $B$, each of mass $m$, lie on a frictionless horizontal track. Initially, $A$ moves to the right with velocity $u>0$, while $B$ is at rest. They undergo a one-dimensional, perfectly elastic collision.

(a) Starting from conservation of momentum and kinetic energy, derive the velocities $v_A$ and $v_B$ after the collision.

(b) Explain why each conservation law applies.

## 2

A ball of mass $m$ moves to the right with speed $u>0$ and strikes a fixed vertical wall. It rebounds to the left with the same speed. Neglect the gravitational impulse during the collision. Take rightward as positive.

(a) Find the impulse exerted by the wall on the ball.

(b) Taking only the ball as the system, determine whether its momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. Explain your answers.

(c) Find the impulse exerted by the ball on the wall.

(d) The two impulses are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Does this imply that the momentum of the ball alone is conserved? Explain.

## 3

Two blocks lie on a frictionless horizontal track. Block $A$ has mass $m$ and initially moves to the right with velocity $u>0$. Block $B$ has mass $3m$ and is initially at rest. They undergo a one-dimensional, perfectly elastic collision.

(a) Find the velocity of each block after the collision.

(b) Find the impulse exerted by $A$ on $B$.

## 4

A bullet of mass $m$ moves horizontally with speed $u$ and embeds itself in a stationary block of mass $M$, suspended by a string. After the collision, the block and bullet swing upward together, rising through a vertical height $h$.

The collision is brief, so the gravitational impulse during the collision may be neglected. Neglect resistance during the subsequent swing, and assume that the string remains taut.

(a) Express the bullet's initial speed $u$ in terms of $m$, $M$, $g$, and $h$.

(b) Identify which conservation laws apply during the collision and which apply during the upward swing. Explain why.

## 5

A projectile of total mass $3m$ reaches the highest point of its trajectory while moving horizontally to the right with speed $u$. It then explodes into two fragments of masses $m$ and $2m$. Neglect the gravitational impulse during the explosion.

Take $+x$ to the right and $+y$ upward. Immediately after the explosion, the fragment of mass $m$ has velocity

$$
\mathbf{v}_m=3u\hat{\mathbf{x}}+4u\hat{\mathbf{y}}.
$$

(a) Find the velocity of the fragment of mass $2m$ immediately after the explosion.

(b WY) Find the increase in the total kinetic energy of the system.

(c) Explain the source of this additional kinetic energy.

## 6

A disk of radius $R=0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates about a fixed central axis. Its angular position is

$$
\theta(t)=t^3,
$$

where $\theta$ is measured in radians and $t$ in seconds. Take counterclockwise as positive.

At $t=2.0\,\mathrm{s}$:

(a) Find the angular velocity and angular acceleration of the disk.

(b) Find the speed of a point on the rim.

(c) Find the tangential acceleration, centripetal acceleration, and total acceleration of that point. Describe their directions.

(d) For a point at distance $R/2$ from the axis, compare its angular velocity, angular acceleration, speed, and acceleration components with those of the point on the rim.

## 7

A uniform thin rod of mass $M$ and length $L$ is attached at one end to a frictionless hinge and can rotate in a vertical plane. It is released from rest in a horizontal position.

(a) Use

$$
I=\int r^2\,dm
$$

to derive the rod's moment of inertia about the hinge.

(b) Find its angular acceleration immediately after release.

(c) Find its angular speed when it first reaches the vertically downward position.

## 8

Two blocks of masses $m$ and $2m$ hang on opposite sides of a pulley, connected by a light, inextensible string. The pulley is a uniform disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$, so its moment of inertia is

$$
I=\frac12(2m)R^2=mR^2.
$$

The pulley rotates about a fixed, frictionless axle. The string does not slip on the pulley. The system is released from rest.

Let $T_1$ be the tension on the side of the block of mass $m$, and $T_2$ the tension on the side of the block of mass $2m$.

(a) Draw or describe the free-body diagrams for both blocks and the pulley, and write their equations of motion.

(b) Find the magnitude of the blocks' acceleration.

(c) Find $T_1$ and $T_2$.

## 9

A uniform solid cylinder of mass $M$ and radius $R$ is released from rest on a fixed incline at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal. It rolls down without slipping. Its moment of inertia about its central axis is

$$
I_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac12MR^2.
$$

Neglect air resistance and rolling resistance. Assume that static friction is sufficient to prevent slipping.

(a) Find the acceleration of the cylinder's center of mass along the incline.

(b) Find the magnitude and direction of the static friction force.

(c) Find the cylinder's speed after its center of mass has descended through a vertical height $h$.

(d) Determine whether mechanical energy is conserved despite the presence of static friction. Explain.

## 10

A uniform thin rod of mass $M$ and length $L$ can rotate without friction in a horizontal plane about a fixed vertical axis through a point $O$. The axis passes through the rod at a distance $L/4$ from one end. The rod is initially at rest.

A clay ball of mass $m$ moves with speed $u$, perpendicular to the rod, and strikes its farther end, a distance $3L/4$ from $O$. The ball sticks to the rod.

Treat the ball as a point mass and neglect other resistive effects during the collision. The rod's moment of inertia about its center of mass is

$$
I_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac1{12}ML^2.
$$

(a) Find the rod's moment of inertia about $O$.

(b) Find the angular velocity of the rod and ball immediately after the collision.

(c) For the rod–ball system, determine whether each of the following is conserved during the collision, and explain why:

- Total linear momentum.
- Total angular momentum about $O$.
- Total kinetic energy.
